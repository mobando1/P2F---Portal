# Diagnostic Class Script

50 minutes · 9 blocks · version v1-2026-08

> The **bold** phrases are said verbatim. They are not suggestions: the system searches the transcript for these exact phrases to know which block each part of the class belongs to. Paraphrase them and that block is lost, and the plan comes out worse.

---

## 1. Framing and consent
*0–3 min · in the student's language*

**"Before we start, let me tell you how this class is going to work."**

Explain that you'll listen more than you talk, that you're sending a written plan afterwards, and that the plan is theirs whether or not they enroll. Ask for recording permission in one casual sentence.

*Feeds:* Recording consent · Plan expectation

## 2. The why
*3–10 min · in the student's language*

**"First tell me: why Spanish, and why now?"**

The "why now" is what separates a browser from a buyer. You already read their form answer — ask them to expand on it, not repeat it.

*Feeds:* goalInTheirWords · Urgency / the trigger

## 3. The cost of not doing it
*10–13 min · in the student's language*

**"And if a year from now you're exactly where you are today, what does that cost you?"**

Ask it gently and then STAY QUIET. The silence is the tool. This is the question that was missing with the student who got excited and disappeared.

*Feeds:* The cost-of-inaction line that opens the plan

## 4. Free speech sample
*13–20 min · in the target language*

**"Now let's switch to Spanish. Cuéntame de tu semana."**

Three uninterrupted minutes. DO NOT CORRECT HERE — you need continuous speech to measure real fluency. Take notes, don't interrupt.

*Feeds:* Fluency baseline · Lexical range · Error pattern

## 5. The real task (role-play)
*20–30 min · in the target language*

**"Hagámoslo de verdad. Yo voy a ser"**

The most important block. Role-play EXACTLY the scenario they wrote in the form. If they said "order dinner for the whole table in Bogotá", you are the waiter. This is where the class delivers value before a dollar changes hands.

*Feeds:* Target-task baseline · The strongest quotes in the plan

## 6. Listening comprehension
*30–35 min · in the target language*

**"Ahora te voy a poner algo a velocidad normal y me cuentas qué entendiste."**

Real native speed, not slowed down. A short clip or you speaking at your normal pace. Ask them to summarize, not translate.

*Feeds:* Comprehension baseline

## 7. Self-diagnosis
*35–40 min · in the student's language*

**"Where did you feel yourself stall?"**

Follow with: "What have you tried before, and why do you think it didn't work?" — that stops you prescribing the method that already failed them, and lets the plan say explicitly why this is different.

*Feeds:* blocker · What method already failed

## 8. Reality check
*40–45 min · in the student's language*

**"Realistically: how many days a week can you give this 50 minutes? And by when do you need it?"**

WITHOUT THIS THE PLAN CANNOT RECOMMEND ANYTHING. Frequency plus deadline is what turns three prices into a single recommendation. Do not skip it for time.

*Feeds:* recommendation.sessionsPerWeek · recommendation.durationWeeks

## 9. Closing and the plan promise
*45–50 min · in the student's language*

**"Let me tell you what I saw."**

Use the closing script almost verbatim. Do not quote a price. The plan carries the recommendation in writing, and in writing it is more persuasive than you are.

*Feeds:* Delivery expectation · The sales moment

---

## Rules

1. Never say "trial class". Always "diagnostic class".
2. Zero selling in minutes 1 through 45.
3. Correct at most three errors live; write down the rest. Being corrected constantly in a first class is why people don't come back.
4. Don't quote a price in the session. The plan carries the recommendation in writing.
5. Send the plan even if the student went cold — especially if they went cold. That's the case the plan exists to recover.

## Closing (almost verbatim)

> Let me tell you what I saw. Today you're at [level, one sentence]. What's holding you back most is [two or three concrete things]. With your goal of [their goal, their words] and the [X] days a week you told me you have, this is roughly a [Y]-month job at [N] times a week.

> All of that goes to your portal in writing before [day], with the week-by-week route. That plan is yours whether you enroll or not — I want you to at least know the road, even if you decide to walk it on your own.

> And one thing I want to be clear about: here, the plan is yours, not a group's. You're not keeping up with whoever's fastest and you're not waiting on whoever's behind. You go at your pace, with me across from you. When you read the plan, if you want us to walk it together, the next step is right there in it.

## Rubric (right after, 60 seconds)

| Skill | Scale | Scored from |
|---|---|---|
| Fluency | 1–10 | Free speech sample |
| Listening comprehension | 1–10 | Listening comprehension |
| Lexical range | 1–10 | Free speech sample |
| Grammatical accuracy | 1–10 | The real task (role-play) |
| Speaking confidence | 1–10 | The real task (role-play) |

**Target task:** Couldn't complete it · With heavy help · Partially · Completed it

Filled in **identically** at classes 4, 8 and 12. The student watches those same bars move on their plan — that is the entire progress system.
